I’m a computational biologist in New York City. I direct the Single Cell Neurogenomics Group, the Lee Lab, in the Center for Disease Neurogenomics at Mount Sinai.

My lab connects single-cell genomics, human genetics and machine learning to understand what makes the brain vulnerable, and resilient, to disease. In practice that means building very large maps: we profile millions of individual cells from human brain tissue, then ask which cell types change in neurodegenerative and neuropsychiatric disorders, what those disorders share, and in which cells the genetic risk variants actually do their work.

I took the scenic route here. I trained as a biomedical engineer at Washington University in St. Louis, spent four years writing software at NCBI, the home of PubMed and GenBank, and then moved to Korea to work on cancer genomics at the National Cancer Center. I did my PhD in Computational Biology & Bioinformatics at Yale with Mark Gerstein, where I contributed to the ENCODE and PCAWG consortia and built STARRPeaker for ENCODE, before coming to New York to start my lab.

Outside the lab I still like building things for the web, which is why this site exists.

{% include bullet.html line="about" scale="sm" %} What my lab works on

  • {% include bullet.html letter="1" color="var(--line-red)" %}

    Population-scale single-cell atlases

    Mapping how every cell type in the brain changes across disorders, and which changes they have in common.

  • {% include bullet.html letter="2" color="var(--line-red)" %}

    Microglia and brain immunity

    How the brain’s own immune cells shift with aging and in Alzheimer’s disease.

  • {% include bullet.html letter="3" color="var(--line-red)" %}

    Cell-type-specific genetics

    Finding the cells where genetic risk variants for brain disorders act.

  • {% include bullet.html letter="4" color="var(--line-green)" %}

    Machine learning

    Methods such as PASCode and iBrainMap that pull disease signals out of millions of single cells.

  • {% include bullet.html letter="5" color="var(--line-green)" %}

    Gene regulation and networks

    How regulatory elements and gene networks shape cell identity, going back to STARRPeaker for ENCODE.
